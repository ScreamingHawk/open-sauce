recipe:
  servings: 6 adults
  prep: 20 minutes
  cook: 4½–5 hours, plus 30 minutes resting
  ingredients:
    - name: lamb forequarter roast
      qty: 2.5 kilograms
    - name: roasting potatoes
      qty: 2 kilograms
    - name: garlic
      qty: 6 cloves
    - name: fresh rosemary
      qty: 3 sprigs
    - name: olive oil
      qty: 3 tablespoons
    - name: salt
      qty: 2 teaspoons
    - name: ground black pepper
      qty: 1 teaspoon
    - name: water or stock
      qty: 1 cup
  directions:
    - Take lamb out of the fridge 30 minutes before cooking
    - Set oven to Roast at 160°C
    - Pat lamb dry, then rub with 1 tablespoon olive oil, salt, pepper, crushed garlic and chopped rosemary
    - Put lamb in a deep roasting pan, fat side up, and pour water or stock into the bottom
    - Insert oven temperature probe into the thickest part of the meat without touching bone, and set the target to 92°C
    - Cover pan tightly with foil and put lamb in the oven — this is 0:00 on the cooking clock
    - At 2:45, peel potatoes if desired, cut into even 5-centimetre pieces and boil in salted water for 10 minutes
    - At 3:00, drain potatoes, shake them in the pot to rough up the edges and coat with remaining olive oil
    - Remove foil, baste lamb, arrange potatoes around it and increase oven to Roast at 200°C
    - Roast uncovered for another 60–90 minutes, turning potatoes after 45 minutes
    - Start checking at 4:00; lamb is ready when the probe reads 90–92°C and a fork twists easily in the meat
    - If lamb is ready before potatoes are golden, move it to a board and leave potatoes in the oven
    - Rest lamb loosely covered with foil for 30 minutes before pulling apart or carving
---

A slow roast for tender, pull-apart lamb and crunchy potatoes. The temperature probe decides when the lamb is ready; the clock is a guide.

For a 2.5 kilogram forequarter, plan to serve about **5 hours after it goes into the oven**, including resting time. Allow another 30 minutes beforehand to take the chill off the meat.

If the probe has not reached 90°C after 4½ hours, cover the lamb loosely with foil and keep roasting. Check every 15 minutes. Forequarter has plenty of connective tissue, so it needs the high internal temperature to become tender.
